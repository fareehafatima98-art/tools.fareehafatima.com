## Questions he may ask that Sanu didn't

**"Why sales, after medicine?"**
The honest arc, not a polished one. Medicine taught you to hold a conversation where the other
person is anxious and needs the truth. Founding taught you that the bottleneck is almost never
the product. Sales is where those two meet.

**"What did you actually do yourself on the plan you sent me?"**
He read that page eight times. He's technical and ex-Google. If he asks how it was made, answer
straight: you used AI tooling to build and render it, the same way you built your MVP, and
every claim in it came from fore ai's own published material because you set that rule. Then
prove ownership by discussing the substance — the infosec stall, POCs over demos, founder hours
as the constraint. Those are judgments, not generated text. Own the tooling; it's evidence you
work like someone who has to be three people at once.

**"What would you need from me?"**
Be specific and modest: time in the first month. Shadowing calls, not being handed a list. And
a fast answer when you're blocked.

**"What are you not good at?"**
Real answer, not a humblebrag. You haven't carried a quota. You're learning the technical depth
of eval tooling. You'd want him in the first closes.

**"What else are you looking at?"**
Don't invent leverage you don't have. *"I have conversations running, but this is the one I've
done the most work on."* True, and stronger than a bluff he could call.

## The commercial conversation

You want full-time. They proposed a trial. **Don't accept it as framed and hope. Shape it.**

**Get the conversion bar named.** *"What would need to be true at the end of the trial for it
to become permanent?"* If he can't answer, propose one: replies generated, meetings booked, a
US sequence tested with data. A trial with a stated bar is a job with a start date. A trial
without one is an audition that can drift indefinitely.

**Propose the scope yourself.** The US versus Europe gap is the obvious candidate. You've
already sent Sanu three hypotheses about it. *"If it were me, I'd spend the first month on the
US gap specifically, because it's measurable and it's the thing you told Sanu you're stuck on."*

**Name a number.** Don't ask what they pay. Say what you want, then let him respond. If you
genuinely don't know the market rate, say: *"I'd rather you tell me what's normal for this
stage and I'll tell you if it works."* That's honest and keeps you from anchoring low.

**Ask about the shape.** Contract or employment. Hours expected. Whether the trial is paid
(it should be). What happens to notice if it converts.
