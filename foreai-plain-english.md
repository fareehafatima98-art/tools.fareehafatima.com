## First, the words

**POC — "proof of concept"** *(say "pee-oh-see" or "a pilot")*
A short trial where the customer actually uses the product on their own real work, with an
agreed test of whether it worked. Not a sales demo — the customer's own hands, their own data,
usually 2-4 weeks.

Why it matters for fore ai: nobody buys testing software because it looked nice in a demo.
They try it on their own AI system, see whether it catches real problems, then buy. So the
thing that predicts revenue isn't "how many demos did we do" — it's **"how many POCs are
running, and did we agree upfront what success looks like."**

If you agree the success measure *before* the trial starts, you get a decision at the end. If
you don't, the trial just fizzles and nobody says no. That's why my plan says
*"POC playbook: entry criteria, success metric, 2-week clock."* In plain words: who qualifies
to start a trial, what counts as it working, and a deadline so it can't drift.

---

**"The infosec stall"** *(infosec = information security)*
A deal that is going well, then gets sent to the customer's security team for review, and
stops dead for weeks or months.

It happens because fore ai's customers are banks and big companies — UBS, Google, Uber. Before
any new software touches their systems, security has to approve it. They send a long
questionnaire: where's the data stored, who can see it, are you certified.

The stall isn't a "no". It's a delay that kills momentum, and deals die of old age.

The fix in my plan: **a one-page security answer, written in advance.** SOC 2 certification,
VPC deployment, data handling — all in a document you hand over the moment security appears,
instead of scrambling for two weeks. That's *"pre-empt the infosec stall."*

**SOC 2** = a security certification companies pay auditors for. Proof you handle data
properly. fore ai has it.
**VPC deployment** = the software can run inside the customer's own private cloud, so their
data never leaves their walls. Banks care enormously about this.

**"Founder-led motion"**
The founder is still personally doing the selling. Asheem is on every call.

It works at first because nobody knows the product like he does. It stops working because
there's only one of him. **Growth is limited by how many hours he has.** That's the sentence
that matters, and it's the entire reason this job exists.

**Top of the funnel**
Picture a funnel: lots of people at the wide top, few customers at the narrow bottom.

- **Top** = finding people and getting them to reply. Cold email, LinkedIn, lists.
- **Middle** = calls, demos, trials.
- **Bottom** = closing, contracts, money.

**You have run the top end to end.** You have not yet done the bottom in tech. That's the
honest line, and it's fine.

---

**Pipeline**
All the potential deals in progress, and roughly what they're worth. "Owning the pipeline"
means you're responsible for there being enough of them.

**Quota**
A sales target you're held to, usually revenue per quarter. You haven't carried one yet.

**ACV — "average contract value"**
What a typical customer pays per year. £5k a year and £200k a year are completely different
jobs, so it's worth asking.

**Sales cycle**
How long from first conversation to signed. Two weeks or nine months changes everything about
how you work.

**Trigger**
A public sign that a company might need you right now. For fore ai: they're hiring AI
engineers, or shipping AI features fast. Better than emailing random companies.

**Objection map**
A written list of every reason people say no, and the best answer to each. You build it by
listening to real calls. Most first sellers never bother, which is why it's in my plan.

**SE — sales engineer**
The technical person who joins calls to answer deep questions. A company hiring SEs is
probably shipping fast, which is a trigger.

**Head of QA — quality assurance**
The person responsible for making sure software works before customers see it. For fore ai,
often the buyer, because AI systems break in ways normal testing can't catch.

**Qualified demo**
A demo with someone who could actually buy, not just someone curious. Ten qualified demos beat
fifty curious ones.
