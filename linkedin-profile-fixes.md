## The core problem

Your outbound says: *I want to be your founding AE.*
Your profile says: *I am the co-founder and CEO of my own startup, and I'm hiring a CTO.*

Every prospect who looks you up hits that contradiction with nothing to resolve it. Asheem
and Anthony both viewed you. Neither replied.

Fix the contradiction and the same emails do more work.

---

## 1. Headline (5 minutes, highest leverage)

It's the one line that travels everywhere — search results, comments, connection requests,
the "someone viewed your profile" notification.

Right now: `Co-Founder @foundrfrnd`

Pick based on what's actually true for you:

**If you're looking to move into a founding AE seat:**
> Founding AE looking for the right early-stage team | Co-Founder @foundrfrnd | I've done the outbound, not just studied it

**If you're doing both and want to be straight about it:**
> Co-Founder @foundrfrnd | Open to a founding AE seat | Built the pipeline before I asked anyone to hire me for it

**If you want the medicine-to-startups arc doing the work:**
> Doctor turned founder turned seller | Co-Founder @foundrfrnd | Looking for a founding AE seat

The honest question underneath: are you leaving foundrfrnd, or running both? Founders will
ask this on the first call. Decide the answer before the headline implies one.

## 5. The CTO hiring post — worth reconsidering

Your public post specifies **"Female, Lahore PK-based preferred, 25 y/o and above."**

Two issues, one practical, one legal:

- It's sitting on the profile of every US, UK and EU founder you're currently emailing.
  In those markets, advertising a role by gender and age is unlawful, and founders there
  will read it as a red flag about how you'd hire on their team.
- It's the second thing a visitor sees after your headline.

You can state the same preferences in DMs when someone applies. On a public post aimed at
a Western startup audience, it costs you more than it gets you. I'd edit or archive it.

Low enough that founders notice, and it caps your reach — LinkedIn shows your posts and
profile to your network first.

Fix it passively while you do outreach: connect with every founder you email, everyone who
views you, and people in the YC and early-stage founder orbit. 500 is the threshold where

## 7. Your content is doing the wrong job

Recent posts are foundrfrnd marketing, six of them ending in the same line: *"4 minutes,
free, 9 criteria."* That reads as a company selling a product.

A founder considering you as their first seller wants evidence you can **sell**, not that
you can market your own tool. Your best post by far is the MVP-deletion one — honest,
specific, no CTA. That's the register that earns replies.

Worth writing: what you learned sending 400 cold emails. What worked, what didn't, what
you'd do differently. That post *is* your application, and every founder you're emailing
is exactly its audience.
