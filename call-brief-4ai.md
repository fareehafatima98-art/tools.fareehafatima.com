## C. The three landmines

**"Have you closed anything in tech?"**
> No. I've closed in a regulated environment outside tech, and I've built and run the top of
> the funnel here. Closing tech deals is the specific thing I want to learn, and I'd rather
> learn it somewhere the founder is still in the room for the first ones.

**"Why leave your own company?"**
> I'm not leaving it, it's a side project now. Running it taught me I'm better at pipeline
> than at product, and I'd rather do that for a company that needs it than half-do it for
> my own.

**"You've never done this job before."**
> Correct. So rather than tell you I could, I built a 90-day plan for companies before they
> ever spoke to me. That's the closest thing to a work sample I can offer.

Never fill the silence after these. Answer, stop, let him respond.

## D. Questions to ask him

Ask these. They're the difference between a candidate and a seller.

**On the role**
1. Is this hire for outbound, or to close inbound that's already coming in?
2. Who is selling today, and what would they do with that time back?
3. Is there a playbook, or am I writing it?

**On the business**
4. How would you define the ICP right now, and how confident are you in it?
5. What's the sales cycle and rough ACV?
6. What's the most common reason a deal stalls today?

**On the bar**
7. What does good look like at day 90? What number would make this obviously working?
8. Has anyone tried this seat before, and what happened?

**The brave one, near the end**
9. I haven't closed in tech yet. What would you need to see from me to get comfortable with
   that?

Question 9 does more for you than any answer you give. It shows you can name an objection
and ask for the path through it, which is the job.
